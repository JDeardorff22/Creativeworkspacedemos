⬆️ Before

Steps Before Create Studio

1

Brand Strategy & Brief Creation

👥 10–12 affected

Brand teams define the creative direction, claims, target audience, and collection scope for upcoming product launches. For the current cycle (JFM 27), hundreds of SKUs are entering the pipeline targeting August/September e-content readiness. This is the starting point for all downstream content work.

What Happens in This Step

  • Brand teams identify which SKUs need new or refreshed content
  • Creative direction is defined — claims, audience, tone, key differentiators
  • Briefs are written in varied formats (no standard template exists across brands)
  • Regulatory and legal review of claims begins (many codes and considerations)
  • Collection scope determined — hero SKU selected, variation count estimated
  • GEO/LLM optimization guidelines should be incorporated at this stage (but often aren't available yet)
⚠️ Pain Points
  • Hundreds of SKUs entering pipeline simultaneously — no capacity planning or prioritization tool
  • GEO/LLM optimization playbook not rolled out in time — teams write copy without it, then must rework later
  • No standardized brief format across brands — each team does it differently, creating downstream confusion
  • Marketing copy takes significant time due to many codes and regulatory considerations
  • No system to surface which SKUs are "ready to start" vs. "blocked on inputs"
  • Brief content often duplicates information that must be manually re-entered into production tools
2

Hero SKU Content Creation (BeautyHouse / Agency)

👥 10–12 affected

BeautyHouse (external agency) creates the hero SKU content — the "gold standard" asset from which all variations will be derived. Sometimes this includes the Key Visual (KV). The hero is one representative SKU per collection that sets the creative benchmark for the remaining 12+ SKUs.

What Happens in This Step

  • BeautyHouse receives the brief and begins creative development
  • Hero SKU imagery (packshots, lifestyle, secondary images) produced
  • Key Visual (KV) sometimes created as a companion hero asset
  • Multiple review rounds between brand team and agency
  • Final hero deliverables handed off — format/structure unclear (PSD? Flat? Layered?)
  • Hero becomes the template/reference for all CMG variations downstream
⚠️ Pain Points
  • Full dependency on external agency — no self-serve capability for brand teams
  • Unclear handoff format — are deliverables PSD with layers? Flat files? What metadata accompanies them?
  • No visibility into agency timeline or status — brand teams wait passively
  • Cannot start variation work in parallel — everything is sequential
  • Agency bottleneck means one late hero delays the entire collection (12+ SKUs)
  • No documented mapping of what BeautyHouse delivers vs. what downstream systems expect
3

Artwork & CIC Process (Trident System)

👥 10–12 affected

Renders, secondary images, and MOIs are produced through the CIC (Creative in Context) pipeline using the Trident artwork system. This step involves coordination between RBU, BFO, and brand teams — and has a hard system capacity constraint that limits throughput.

What Happens in This Step

  • Trident artwork system receives production orders (POAs)
  • System processes approximately 10 POAs per day (hard throughput cap)
  • Renders, secondary images, and initial MOIs produced
  • Coordination required between RBU (Regional Business Unit), BFO, and brand teams
  • Priority brands get CIC revisions first — lower-priority brands cascade behind
  • Last year, a tracking list showed each SKU's position in the artwork pipeline — users want this back
⚠️ Pain Points
  • Process is excessively long and frequently late — no predictable timeline
  • Unclear ownership — nobody knows who's responsible for each step across RBU, BFO, and brand
  • Trident throughput cap: ~10 POAs/day creates a hard ceiling everyone competes against
  • Priority brand revisions cascade delays to ALL lower-priority brands in the queue
  • SKU tracking visibility that existed last year no longer exists — teams are flying blind
  • No notification system when artwork is complete or blocked
"I'm already scared of looking at that path"
"Trident can only touch, I think, like 10 POAs a day or something like that... if you start to have revisions for some of the brands in priority, it kind of trickles down to the ones behind."
4

CMG Variation (Hero → 12+ SKU Variations)

👥 8–10 affected

After the hero SKU content is finalized, it's handed to CMG (Content Management Group) to produce variations for the full collection — typically 12+ SKUs per hero. This is an entirely manual process involving multiple rounds of back-and-forth review with no automation or templating.

What Happens in This Step

  • Hero SKU deliverables sent to CMG with variation instructions
  • CMG manually adapts hero content for each SKU in the collection
  • Variation includes: copy changes, claim adjustments, imagery swaps per SKU
  • Multiple review rounds between brand team and CMG
  • Each round of feedback must cycle through CMG's queue before returning
  • Once all 12+ variations are approved, collection can proceed to optimization
⚠️ Pain Points
  • Takes 1+ month of back-and-forth — blocks entire collection from being content-complete
  • No automation whatsoever — fully manual process for every single variation
  • No templating — each variation starts from scratch even when changes are minimal
  • No self-serve capability — brand teams cannot do this themselves
  • No status visibility during the waiting period — "where is my variation?"
  • Sequential process — cannot parallelize any part of it
  • One CMG resource handles multiple brands — competing priorities
5

GEO / SEO / LLM Optimization

👥 10–12 affected

Copy must be optimized for both traditional SEO (organic search rank on retailer sites) AND the newer LLM/Alexa algorithm — which are confirmed to be two completely different ranking systems with different signals. This step also involves auditing backend search terms to prevent AI hallucinations.

What Happens in This Step

  • Copy reviewed against GEO playbook (finalized ~2 weeks prior to workshop)
  • SEO keywords validated for retailer organic search performance
  • LLM readability check — ensuring content can be scraped by Alexa/ChatGPT
  • Backend search terms audited for outdated/conflicting claims
  • Natural language optimization for conversational AI queries
  • Dual validation: copy must score well on BOTH traditional SEO AND LLM algorithms
  • Sean (GEO expert) currently does manual reviews — single point of dependency
⚠️ Pain Points
  • Dual algorithm conflict: SEO optimization and LLM optimization are different systems — content optimized for one may not perform on the other
  • No tooling exists to validate content against both algorithms simultaneously
  • Teams must now "double-optimize" every piece of copy manually
  • Backend search terms contain outdated claims causing Alexa to hallucinate incorrect product info
  • No automated detection of stale backend metadata vs. current approved content
  • Single-person dependency (Sean) for GEO reviews — doesn't scale
  • Playbook timing: rolled out too late for teams already deep in JFM 27 pipeline
  • No clean-room testing for Alexa — all results polluted by personal shopping history
"The SEO organic rank might be different than how you show up on Alexa. There are two different algorithms that we need to [optimize for]."
"In the back end, there's an old claim in there versus what we're saying a new claim on the site and then it's causing Alexa to hallucinate."
6

MOI (Main Operating Image) Creation

👥 10–12 affected

Teams create Main Operating Images (MOIs) — the primary product images on retailer PDPs. These often include visual cues, banners, or badges to drive conversion. Each retailer has different acceptance criteria, and enforcement is inconsistent even within a single retailer.

What Happens in This Step

  • MOIs designed with visual cues/banners (e.g., "New!", scent badges, benefit callouts)
  • Two versions created per MOI: one WITH visual cues, one WITHOUT (fallback)
  • Submitted to Amazon, Walmart, and Target via respective publishing channels
  • Automated acceptance/rejection processes run on each retailer
  • Rejections may be overridden through separate manual channels
  • Active issue sheet maintained with Amazon regarding inconsistent enforcement
  • Epoch testing may be run on hero MOI before variating across full collection
⚠️ Pain Points
  • Acceptance/rejection is non-deterministic: same asset accepted on one SKU, rejected on another within the same retailer
  • Inconsistent across Amazon, Walmart, and Target — no unified rule set
  • Forces 2x workload — must create fallback version for every single MOI
  • No deterministic rule set exposed to publishers — can't predict what will pass
  • Workaround requires "separate channel" to manually override rejections
  • Leadership asks "update all MOIs to Wild" without understanding rejection rates
  • No formal A/B testing process — "epoch testing" referenced but not tooled
"It really seems to come down to a SKU level... there's times where we will submit the initial MOI, it'll get rejected through the automated process, and then we're able to work through a separate channel to get it accepted."

ICS Create Studio — Content Creation & Editing Platform

👥 10–12 in hands-on session

Create Studio is positioned between the upstream planning/production steps and the downstream publishing/monitoring steps. It can accelerate or replace several of the manual processes above — particularly around copy generation, image editing, variation creation, and content validation.

✍️ Copywriter (AI Generation)

AI-powered copy creation using structured prompts, brand guidelines, and reference documents. Reduces dependency on external agencies for initial copy drafts.

Addresses: Step 1 (brief→copy), Step 4 (variations), Step 5 (optimization)

🖼️ Touchup / Layers

Full image compositing canvas with layer management, text on curves, AI inpainting, and image manipulation. Enables self-serve image editing without Photoshop.

Addresses: Step 2 (hero adaptation), Step 4 (variation imagery), Step 6 (MOI creation)

🎨 Image Generation

AI-powered image creation from reference images and text prompts. Can generate variations from a hero reference, reducing CMG dependency.

Addresses: Step 4 (hero→variation), Step 6 (MOI alternatives)

📄 Brief Upload + Prepopulation

Upload brief documents with AI extraction to auto-populate form fields (brand name, claims, audience). Eliminates redundant data entry.

Addresses: Step 1 (brief intake), reducing manual re-entry

📋 Brand Guidelines Integration

Embedded brand guidelines ensure all generated content is on-brand. Guidelines injected into AI prompts automatically when enabled.

Addresses: Steps 1–6 (brand compliance throughout)

🏪 BrandStore / DAM Integration

Pull approved assets directly from BrandStore without download/re-upload. DamBrowser component enables search, filter, and import within the tool.

Addresses: Step 2 (importing hero assets), Step 3 (accessing renders)

🔍 GEO Playbook Validation (Future)

AI-powered validation of copy against SEO + LLM best practices. Reduces single-person dependency on GEO expert for manual reviews.

Addresses: Step 5 (dual algorithm optimization)

🔄 Content Variation at Scale (Future)

Self-serve variation of hero content into 12+ SKUs using AI + templates. Could collapse 1-month CMG cycle into days.

Addresses: Step 4 (CMG bottleneck elimination)

Steps After Create Studio

7

PRaaS Registration (Rights as a Service)

👥 10–12 affected

Every asset created must be registered in PRaaS (P&G Rights as a Service) for rights tracking and compliance. This ensures the organization knows what was created, by whom, when, and what rights are attached. Adapt Studio does this automatically; Create Studio currently does not.

What Happens in This Step

  • User exports/downloads final asset from Create Studio
  • User must manually navigate to PRaaS and register the asset
  • Registration includes: creator identity, creation date, source tool, asset type, rights scope
  • Adapt Studio (sister tool) does this automatically on export — parity expected
  • Batch exports require individual registration per asset
  • Unregistered assets create compliance risk if used in market
⚠️ Pain Points
  • Manual step that users forget or don't know about — no prompt or reminder
  • No automation — parity gap with Adapt Studio which auto-registers
  • Compliance risk if assets are used without registration
  • Separate system with separate login — context switch required
  • No way to know which Create Studio assets are registered vs. not
  • Batch exports multiply the manual effort (N assets = N registrations)
8

PIM Submission & Approval

👥 10–12 affected

Finalized content is submitted to PIM (Product Information Management) — the system of record for all product content. PIM is where content is considered "official" and from which all retailer publishing flows. Once content is approved in PIM, it's expected to be the definitive truth.

What Happens in This Step

  • Copy, images, A+ modules, and metadata submitted to PIM
  • Internal approval workflow within PIM (varies by brand/region)
  • Content marked as "approved" / "ready to publish" in PIM
  • PIM becomes the source of truth — everything downstream references it
  • Backend search terms, category assignments, and attributes set
  • Soft conversion timing coordinated to avoid suppression
⚠️ Pain Points
  • Content marked correct/complete in PIM but doesn't match what's live on retailer sites
  • A failed push 6+ months ago produced NO alerting — system silently failed
  • No retry mechanism when a PIM→retailer push fails
  • No reconciliation loop to verify content actually landed on the live site
  • No notification to content owners that a publish was incomplete or reverted
  • No staging environment or pre-publish validation before going live
  • Pushing updated content for soft conversions risks taking SKUs offline
"It's right in PIM, like why is it wrong? ...oh yeah, six months ago we pushed it, the thing was..."
"We don't want things being suppressed, going offline, we want to make sure we're doing it right."
9

Retailer Publishing (PIM → Amazon / Walmart / Target)

👥 10–12 affected

Content flows from PIM to retailer PDPs via publishing pipelines. Each retailer (Amazon, Walmart, Target) has its own pipeline, acceptance criteria, and suppression rules. The publishing step is where content goes from "internal truth" to "consumer-facing reality" — and where most silent failures occur.

What Happens in This Step

  • PIM pushes approved content to Amazon Vendor Central, Walmart Retail Link, Target syndication
  • Each retailer runs its own acceptance/rejection checks on incoming content
  • Images validated against retailer-specific size, format, and content rules
  • Copy validated against character limits and category requirements
  • Content either publishes to live PDP or enters a rejection/suppression state
  • Suppressed content can take SKU offline entirely — revenue impact is immediate
  • No visibility into pipeline status once content leaves PIM
⚠️ Pain Points
  • No mechanism to compare PIM (source of truth) vs. what's actually live on the PDP
  • Root-cause analysis of publishing failures is impossible without manual SKU-by-SKU inspection
  • Content owners find out about failures weeks or months later — no alerting
  • Silent failures: system doesn't surface when a push doesn't land
  • Pushing updated content risks triggering suppression rules and taking SKUs offline
  • No staging environment to validate before going live
  • Ownership gap: brand teams say "it's right in PIM" — customer teams say "it's wrong on site" — nobody owns the gap
  • Current workflow: someone notices it's wrong → manual PDP check → manual PIM check → shrug
"I get the same pings... we're like, it's right in PIM, like why is it wrong?"
10

Live Site Monitoring & Content Scoring

👥 10–12 affected

Corporate data analytics has built prototype dashboards that scrape live PDP content from Amazon, Walmart, and Target. These dashboards score content against retailer-specific criteria and show performance metrics. Currently in prototype stage — no one is onboarded yet, and refreshes are ad hoc.

What Happens in This Step

  • Live PDP data scraped into a data lake (Amazon, Walmart, Target separately)
  • Content scored against retailer-specific criteria (completeness, quality, compliance)
  • Performance metrics captured: click share, conversion share, relevancy score
  • Content change detection: "major/minor change" tracking partially built
  • Brand teams compare current scores to historical baselines
  • Cross-retailer parity checks (is same SKU consistent across 3 retailers?)
  • Dashboard refresh happens ad hoc — no regular cadence established
⚠️ Pain Points
  • Three siloed dashboards — no unified cross-retailer view for a single SKU
  • Refresh is ad hoc — no regular cadence, making content score trending unreliable
  • No one is onboarded to the dashboards yet (prototype stage)
  • Cannot identify content parity issues across channels without checking 3 separate tools
  • When scores change, no visibility into WHAT content change caused it (positive or negative)
  • Users want to influence which metrics are displayed (glance views, conversion rate, etc.)
  • Architecture doesn't support a consolidated cross-retailer data model
  • No way to pressure-test different MOI formats based on actual performance data
"a game changer" — "our band 5's asked for that a lot"
"pressure test different MOI formats based on how well it performs"
11

A+ / Enhanced Content Maintenance

👥 10–12 affected

Enhanced content modules on PDPs (A+ on Amazon, Rich Media on Walmart) are maintained over time. Historically image-heavy, this content is now recognized as invisible to LLMs — creating a strategic gap as AI-powered shopping (Alexa, ChatGPT) grows to 300M+ users.

What Happens in This Step

  • A+ content modules reviewed for relevance and accuracy over time
  • Existing image-heavy A+ content identified as "invisible" to LLMs
  • New text-based A+ templates needed: comparison tables, FAQ sections, claims
  • Brand Story module identified as a quick win (brand-wide, one-time setup)
  • Content must be structured so Alexa/ChatGPT can scrape and correctly cite
  • Backend search terms audited against current approved claims
  • Old claims causing Alexa hallucinations must be identified and removed
⚠️ Pain Points
  • Years of A+ content investment (image-based) are invisible to Alexa, ChatGPT, and LLMs (300M+ Alexa users)
  • Must rebuild with text-based structures — massive rework of existing content
  • Brand Story module is a quick win but hasn't been prioritized or rolled out
  • Outdated backend search terms conflict with current claims → AI hallucinations
  • No standardized checklist/audit process for ensuring backend terms are current
  • No tooling to check if existing A+ content is LLM-readable before publishing
  • Cannot test how Alexa responds to your content without personal purchase history bias
"Historically, a lot of it image-based... cannot be read by Alexa or ChatGPT that scrape your site."
"The trickiest thing is the incognito. You have to be signed in to use Alexa."
12

Leadership Reporting & Stakeholder Education

👥 10–12 affected

Leadership receives reports on content performance, content completeness, and brand presence across retailers. They make asks based on these reports without fully understanding the constraints of the content pipeline, retailer acceptance rules, and system limitations.

What Happens in This Step

  • Leadership reviews content scores and brand performance metrics
  • Asks are generated: "update all MOIs to Wild," "why doesn't top 100 look perfect?"
  • Brand teams must explain constraints, rejection rates, and inconsistencies reactively
  • No structured education exists to set realistic expectations proactively
  • Alexa/LLM visibility checks done by leadership return personalized (biased) results
  • Gap between "what leadership expects" and "what's actually achievable" never formally closed
⚠️ Pain Points
  • Leadership makes asks without understanding retailer-specific limitations and rejection rates
  • "Update all MOIs to Wild" — doesn't understand non-deterministic acceptance
  • "Why doesn't top 100 look perfect?" — doesn't understand silent publish failures and suppression risk
  • No structured education pushed UP to leadership — all explanations are reactive
  • Leadership checking Alexa performance gets personalized results (no clean-room testing)
  • Expectations gap creates frustration on both sides — brand teams feel blamed for systemic issues
  • No dashboard or report that surfaces constraints alongside performance data

💡 Bottom Line

The eContent workflow has 12 distinct steps with pain points at every stage. Create Studio addresses steps 1–6 directly (content creation) and indirectly supports steps 7–12 (through better content quality upstream). The primary gap is not capability — it's adoption. The team's most urgent need is visibility and tracking across the end-to-end pipeline, followed by speed improvements at the CMG variation bottleneck (Step 4), which Create Studio's AI generation can directly collapse from 1 month to days.
